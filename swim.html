    Where the 1.5 s comes from
    1. 15 m underwater phase — the largest single opportunity.
    2. 15–20 m transition — the breakout into stroke. Currently the main leak.
    3. Backstroke start — under-trained; two sessions a week rarely leave room for it.

    Stroke rate is not a realistic lever at two sessions a week.

    Constraints on the coached sessions

    • Fins mask the problem — they carry you through exactly the metres where time is lost. Push for 15–25 m unassisted; save fins for over-speed and the 35–50 m efforts.
    • Resisted work stays light — light enough that stroke rate holds. Heavy tow builds a slower pattern.
    • Ask for starts — 5–6 backstroke starts in the last ten minutes of Thursday, from late September.
    • Third session from January if pool access allows — uncoached, 30 min of starts, breakouts and 15s.

    Standard test set — every 6–8 weeks, identical protocol

    • 15 m from push
    • 25 m from start
    • 50 m broken

    Log 15 m split, breakout distance and stroke count at every race.

    Taper protocol

    The taper is a gym taper. At two swim sessions a week you carry almost no swimming fatigue — nearly all accumulated load is the four gym days. Cutting swim volume from an already-minimal base costs feel for the water and buys no freshness.

    Tier 1 — Race through (no taper)

    SC Counties · Regional SC · December open · LC Regionals · February standby. Train normally; skip Saturday gym if racing Sunday. These meets are data, not performances.

    Tier 2 — Freshen (5–7 days)

    LC Counties (7) · Derbyshire Masters Open (5, if applicable) · May open (5) · British Masters (7).

    • Pull deadlifts out of the week entirely.
    • Cut all isolation work.
    • Keep power cleans, jumps, bench singles.
    • Swim both sessions unchanged, but come out of the last set on Thursday.

    Tier 3 — Full taper (14 days · 10-day version for GB Champs)

    • Days 14–8: gym volume down ~40%, loads held. No deadlifts. Last squat session at the start of this window.
    • Days 7–4: two short sessions, potentiation only — jumps, med-ball throws, 2–3 bench singles at ~80%. Nothing that grinds. No lower-body eccentrics after day 5.
    • Days 3–0: no gym. Swim as normal minus the final set.

    Do not diet into a taper. Hold intake through taper weeks even though gym expenditure drops. 400 g lighter buys nothing in a 50; feeling flat costs plenty.

    Recalculate pull-up loads off current bodyweight coming out of each taper block — don't resume where you left off.

    Secondary events

    100 back — forces stroke mechanics to hold past the point the 50 allows brute force. If the 100 improves faster than the 50, the problem is speed; if slower, it's mechanics.

    50 free — start and breakout reps under race conditions. Different start, same underwater and transition problem.

    Placement

    • Counties & Regionals: take both.
    • LC Counties, Masters Open, May open: one, not both, and only off the 50 back's day.
    • GB Champs, British Masters, Budapest: 50 back only.

    The 100 back is genuinely fatiguing. If it's scheduled before the 50 back on the same day, skip it or accept a slow 50. Check programmes before entering.

      Competitive context

      25–29 is the first masters band and the hardest — populated by recently-retired club and university swimmers, closer to national senior racing than the general perception of masters swimming.

      26.5 should put you in the mix rather than at the sharp end. Not yet checked against Singapore 2025 results for men's 25–29 50 back.

      The upside of being at the bottom of the band: the 1.5 seconds is a training problem, not an age problem. A returning 25-year-old with eleven months of structure and a specific technical target is the profile that closes that gap.

      Consequence for planning: non-masters racing is more valuable, not less. Counties, Regionals and open LC meets put you against senior swimmers at or above your level — better preparation for a 25–29 final than wide age-band fields. Keep them in even when inconvenient.
